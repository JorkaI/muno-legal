Privacy Policy

Last updated 15 October 2026

muno is a personal vocabulary app. You collect words, and muno turns them into cards with a translation, pronunciation and audio, then schedules when you practise them. This page explains what data that involves, who processes it, how long we keep it, and what you can ask us to do with it. It applies to the muno app and to this website.

The short version

  • We collect what the app needs to work: your email address, the words and decks you save, your settings and progress, and whether you subscribed. Nothing else.
  • Your account and your collection are stored in the European Union, in Frankfurt.
  • When you add a word, the word itself (never anything about you) goes to OpenAI for its translation and to Google or Microsoft for its audio.
  • From version 1.2.0 the app records anonymous usage statistics. They carry no identifier of any kind, and nothing is stored on your phone for them.
  • No advertising, no tracking, no selling of data, no marketing emails.
  • You can delete your account yourself, in the app, at any moment.

What happens when you add a word

This is the one part of muno where text leaves our own servers, so it is worth reading closely.

When you type a word and ask for its translation, the word travels to our server and from there to OpenAI, together with the language it is in and the language or languages to translate into. OpenAI answers whether it is a real word, its dictionary form, its part of speech, its meanings and, for English, a transcription when our own pronunciation dictionary has none. Nothing about you goes with it: not your email address, not your account identifier, not the rest of your collection. We ask OpenAI not to keep the answer. OpenAI may keep the request for up to 30 days to monitor abuse and does not use it to train its models.

This happens when you ask for the translation, even if you then decide not to save the word. The answer is kept for 7 days in a shared draft, so that the next person asking about the same word does not pay for a second answer; the draft records the word, not who asked. Our server also counts the request against your plan's allowance and, if the word was not recognised, checks its spelling on our own server to suggest a correction.

When you save the word, it becomes an entry in a shared dictionary, together with its audio, which Google Cloud Text-to-Speech produces for English, Spanish, German, French, Italian and Portuguese and Microsoft Azure Speech for Ukrainian and Polish. Each of them receives the word alone. The dictionary holds words, not people: no entry records who added it, and every user of muno can see it.

Please do not type personal information into a word or phrase: not your own name, phone number or address, and not anyone else's. It would be treated as a word, sent to the providers above, and could end up in the shared dictionary.

The ready-made decks in the library are our own content. Taking one copies its words into your account.

Where your data is stored

Your account and word collection are stored in our Supabase project in Frankfurt, inside the European Union. The audio for Ukrainian and Polish is produced in Sweden, and our emails are sent from Ireland.

OpenAI, Google Cloud, RevenueCat, Apple and Google's sign-in service are based in the United States, so that processing takes place outside the European Economic Area. Those transfers rely on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework where the provider is certified under it, and otherwise on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses included in our agreements with the provider. You can ask us for a copy of those safeguards.

On your device

The app keeps a few things on your phone so that it opens instantly: the token that keeps you signed in, your settings and practice preferences, the theme, the days and hour of your reminder, an unfinished practice session, and a working copy of your collection's lists. Before an account exists, the choices you make during the first-run setup are kept on the phone until you sign in. None of this is sent anywhere except as described on this page, and signing out removes your account data from the device.

The daily reminder is scheduled by your phone itself. There is no push token and no server involved: nothing leaves the phone for it, and the permission lives in your phone's settings.

The app reads your phone's language once, to pick the interface language, and its region setting, for the statistics above. It does not use the microphone, the camera, your contacts or your location.

How long we keep it

  • Your account and word collection: for as long as your account exists. You can delete it yourself at any moment, in the app (see Deleting your account), or ask us and we will do it.
  • A deleted deck: 30 days in Recently deleted, then removed for good.
  • An email address entered but never confirmed: 7 days.
  • Sign-in sessions: until they end. The sign-in journal: 30 days.
  • Server logs: a short rolling window, never longer than 30 days.
  • Backups of the database: kept for 7 days, then overwritten in turn; a deleted account disappears from them within that time.
  • The shared draft of a translation: 7 days. The shared dictionary: indefinitely, because it is not personal data and is not linked to any account.
  • Usage statistics: 13 months.
  • Your subscription record at RevenueCat: for as long as your account exists; see Deleting your account for what happens after.
  • Purchase records at Apple: Apple retains transaction records under its own terms and retention periods, which we do not control.

Deleting your account

In the app: Settings → Account → Delete account. You confirm, and the account is erased on the spot: every deck, word, schedule, practice day, setting and counter, in every language you were learning, and then the account itself with its sign-in identities and sessions. There is no waiting period, no email is needed, and nothing can be restored afterwards.

What remains, and why:

  • The words you added to the shared dictionary stay in it, because they are not linked to you.
  • The usage statistics are unaffected, because they never identified you.
  • RevenueCat keeps the record of your subscription under your random account identifier, which after the deletion points to no one. Write to us and we will have that record deleted too.
  • Apple keeps its own purchase records under your Apple Account, and Google its own under your Google Account.
  • Entries in the sign-in journal and the server logs expire within 30 days.
  • The database's nightly backups hold the account until they are overwritten, within 7 days.

If you cannot use the app, write to us from the address the account is registered to and we will delete it for you.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask us to:

  • tell you what data we hold about you, and give you a copy of it,
  • correct anything that is wrong,
  • delete your account and everything in it,
  • restrict or object to a particular use of your data, including any use based on our legitimate interests,
  • export your data in a portable format.

The fastest way to delete your account is inside the app itself, as described above. For everything else, write to jgahokidze@gmail.com and we will respond within one month, as the GDPR requires. We will not charge you and we will not ask you to justify the request. To protect your account, we may ask you to write from the address it is registered to.

Nothing muno does relies on your consent, so there is no consent to withdraw inside the app. The permission for reminders belongs to your phone, and you can switch it off in the phone's settings at any time.

We make no automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects about you. The schedule that decides when a word comes back is automatic, but it affects only what the app shows you.

If you are unhappy with how we handled your request, you can complain to the Polish supervisory authority, the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (Prezes Urzędu Ochrony Danych Osobowych), ul. Stawki 2, 00-193 Warsaw, Poland, or to the data protection authority in your own country; in the United Kingdom that is the Information Commissioner's Office.

Security

Everything between the app and our servers travels encrypted. Your rows in the database can be read only by your own account: the database enforces that on every table, and the keys that reach the providers above live only on the server, never inside the app. There are no passwords to leak: the sign-in code works once and expires. We never hold payment details. If a breach ever affected your data in a way that put you at risk, we would tell you and the supervisory authority, as the GDPR requires.

What muno is, and is not

muno is a tool for collecting and practising your own vocabulary. It is not a language course and does not provide professional teaching or translation services. The automatic translations, transcriptions and ready-made decks are generated or curated aids that support your own learning: they can contain mistakes, so check anything that matters. This describes what the service is; it does not limit any rights you have under consumer law. The terms under which the app is licensed to you are Apple's Standard End User License Agreement.
